# Landing page copy frameworks

Use the entry message and the biggest unresolved buying question to select a pattern. Keep framework names out of the page copy.

| What the visitor knows | Lead with |
| --- | --- |
| They recognize a problem | **Problem → consequence → offer.** Name the verified trigger, show one supportable cost, and name the offer. |
| They want an outcome or are comparing options | **Outcome → mechanism.** Name the useful ability or result, then the step that produces it. |
| They came for a specific offer | **Offer → decision detail.** Name the offer and surface the price, eligibility, or timing that determines whether they act. |
| The buyer trigger or awareness is uncertain | **Confirmed offer first.** Name the category and audience. Use a problem-led version when buyer evidence supports it. |

## Turn the difference into a benefit

Name the buyer's actual alternative, the verified difference in the offer, and what that difference lets them see, do, or decide. Show who performs the work. A named method can follow the concrete explanation. If a competitor could make the same claim, look for a specific scope, step, use case, or term. For an implied downside, such as disruption from switching quickly, pair the advantage with the verified requirement that answers the concern.

## Put proof beside the claim

Match proof to the takeaway. A demo supports an observable task. A labeled sample shows what the buyer receives. A quote supports the experience it describes when wording and permission are verified. A case study supports the measured outcome within its buyer context, metric, baseline, timeframe, and scope. If placement could imply a result the evidence did not measure, move the proof or state the limit beside it. When proof is thin, show a verified process or deliverable and narrow the promise.

## Write the action and next states

- **Before the click.** Label the action and give the detail that resolves hesitation, such as a form before scheduling, a fee, or a required commitment. For a form before a scheduler, label the first stage as starting the booking or continuing to scheduling. Reserve booking confirmation for a reserved time.
- **Form or checkout.** Use visible labels, decision-critical terms, and useful hints. Keep fields needed for qualification. Where a surprising field is required, explain its actual purpose.
- **Error or confirmation.** Name the issue and real recovery path. Confirm what happened, who acts next, and any verified timing. Distinguish email prepared from enquiry sent, application received from accepted, and booking started from reserved.

Cut generic lead-ins, hype, and unsupported benefits. Replace them with the actor, action, and verified detail. Keep buying terms and approved buyer language. Use only real deadlines.

## Example

Fictional invoice reporting tool. **Headline:** See which invoices are overdue before your Monday review. **Subhead:** Connect your billing account to get a weekly report grouped by customer and days overdue. **CTA:** Preview the sample report. **Proof caption:** Sample report with fictional invoice data.
