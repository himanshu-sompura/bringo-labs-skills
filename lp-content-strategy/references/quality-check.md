# Landing page copy quality gate

Run on the final draft or during an audit. Read the page against the actual ad, search intent, or outreach message, the discovery brief, verified offer terms, available proof, and the flow after the CTA. For a section edit, run the relevant checks and inspect any adjacent copy the edit affects.

## Decide whether the copy is ready

| Verdict | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| **PASS** | Every applicable check passes. The copy is ready for publication or testing. |
| **REVISE** | A check fails and the available facts support a fix. Make the fix and rerun the affected checks before delivery. |
| **BLOCKED** | A material fact, source, or decision is missing or conflicts with approved copy, and a truthful rewrite cannot resolve it. Provide what can be used and state exactly what would unblock the verdict. |

One material failure is enough to withhold a pass. A fact is material if a different answer could change who acts, what they expect, what they commit to, or whether they trust the central promise. Mark a check outside the scope as N/A. Mark a required check with missing evidence as unverified. Use **BLOCKED** when publishing would depend on that evidence. A pass records editorial readiness. Conversion lift requires a live test.

## Run the checks in order

### 1. Trace the promise that earned the click

Write down what the visitor expects based on the real entry message. Compare it with the hero and primary CTA. **Pass** when the hero acknowledges that expectation and the CTA advances it. Check the offer, audience, promised outcome or asset, and any advertised terms. Matching keywords alone can still hide a change in the offer. If active campaigns make materially different promises, review each against the page and flag where separate first screens may be needed.

### 2. Reconstruct the offer using only the first screen

Read the hero and CTA without consulting the brief. Answer in plain words and point to the line that supports each answer.

- Is this meant for someone in my situation?
- What am I being offered, and what will it help me do or get?
- What happens immediately if I click?

**Pass** when a first-time visitor can answer all three from the visible copy. The headline can carry the problem or outcome if the supporting line names the offer. If you only have text, assess the hero block and verify what actually appears on mobile when a rendered page is available.

### 3. Find a reason to choose this offer

After reading the page, complete this sentence using a fact on the page. “I would choose this over [what I do or use today] because [specific difference] lets me [practical payoff].” Use an alternative the buyer actually considers, such as a spreadsheet, an incumbent, or delaying the decision. **Pass** when the difference matters to this buyer, belongs to this offer, and connects to a useful outcome. If you can only name a feature, explain what it changes for the buyer. If the offer has a relevant advantage without proven exclusivity, state the advantage on its own merits.

### 4. Audit what the buyer will infer from the proof

For each claim that drives the decision, record the **page line → likely buyer takeaway → source → scope the source supports**. Check implied promises as well as explicit numbers. Compare the metric, timeframe, baseline, customer context, and attribution wherever they affect the takeaway. **Pass** when the claim and nearby evidence support the same conclusion. Narrow claims to what the evidence shows. A representative sample shows what a deliverable contains. Label illustrative data. A measured case supports the outcome measured in that case. Where outcome proof is unavailable, show a verified deliverable or process and adjust the promise to match it. A qualifier elsewhere on the page cannot repair an overstated headline.

### 5. Put the decisive objection where the buyer needs the answer

Use prospect replies, sales calls, reviews, or the approved brief to identify the concern most likely to stop a good-fit buyer. If you are inferring it, label it as a hypothesis in the review. Read the headings alone, then the section before the relevant CTA. **Pass** when buyers can find the answer before the commitment it affects. Surface price or pricing basis, eligibility, timing, required effort, or who does the work when it changes that decision. Confirm deadlines, fees, and exclusions against the terms. Use the real term, requirement, or evidence to answer the concern. Move buried answers forward and cut sections that repeat a point without adding evidence or advancing the decision.

### 6. Walk the CTA through to completion

Follow the button to the next screen, then read the form, booking, checkout, or confirmation copy available for review. Check what the visitor gives, what they get, and what happens next. **Pass** when the label and nearby copy accurately describe the immediate step, and each later step keeps that expectation. Keep eligibility questions that protect lead quality. Where a required field may surprise the buyer, explain its actual purpose. A form submission confirms receipt. A booking confirmation requires a reserved time. If the flow is unavailable, review against the documented steps and state what still needs a live check.

## Example of a failed review

A cohort marketing page claims “Fill your next batch 80% faster.” Its case study shows monthly enquiries rose from 120 to 216 over two months. The CTA says “Book a call” and opens a form before the calendar.

- **Proof fails.** The numbers show an 80% increase in monthly enquiries. The source supplies no measure of enrollment speed. Use “Monthly enquiries rose from 120 to 216 over two months” in the case study and ground the hero in the verified offer.
- **CTA fails.** If the calendar appears immediately after submission, use “Start my booking” with “Complete a short form, then choose a time to talk.” Confirm the booking after the visitor reserves a time.

## Retest and deliver

Fix the earliest point where the buyer’s expectation breaks. Rerun any checks the change affects. When revising, return one paste-ready draft in page order and note any material open issue after the copy. When auditing, return the verdict, the exact line or missing detail causing each failure, and the smallest fix. Preserve approved wording and section order where they pass. Flag a conflict if resolving it would change an approved promise, offer, or commitment.

If target buyers are available, show them the real entry message and first screen. Ask them to describe the offer and next step in their own words. Then ask what they would need to see before acting. Use their answers to locate confusion. When testing performance, choose an outcome that reflects the goal, such as qualified bookings or paid enrollments. Track clicks and submissions to diagnose where people drop off.
