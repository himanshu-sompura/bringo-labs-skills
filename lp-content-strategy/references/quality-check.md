# Landing page copy quality gate

Use for audits and before delivering a draft. Compare against the actual entry message, brief, approved terms, proof, and conversion flow. For a section edit, check the affected section and adjacent copy.

| Verdict | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| **PASS** | Every applicable check passes. Flag any live verification outside the review scope. |
| **REVISE** | A material check fails and the known facts support a fix. Rewrite and rerun affected checks before delivering a revision. In an audit, report the verdict for the supplied version. |
| **BLOCKED** | A material fact, permission, or decision is missing or conflicting and the copy cannot be made truthful and usable without it. Give the usable copy and exact blocker. |

Use N/A outside scope. Mark unavailable evidence unverified. A fact is material if it could change who acts, what they expect or commit to, or whether they trust the main promise.

1. **Entry promise.** Compare actual ad or outreach, hero, and CTA. Confirm the same buyer, offer, outcome, and advertised terms. Matching keywords alone can hide a change in promise.
2. **First screen.** Read the hero and CTA without the brief. Point to the wording that answers who it is for, what they get, and what happens next. Assess visible mobile placement only when the rendered page is available.
3. **Reason to choose.** Finish “I would choose this over [real alternative] because [verified difference] lets me [practical payoff].” Use an advantage on its own merits when exclusivity is unverified.
4. **Proof and accuracy.** For decision-driving lines record **copy → likely takeaway → source → supported scope**. Check the implied claim, metric, baseline, timeframe, attribution, and permission where relevant. A caveat elsewhere cannot repair an overstated headline.
5. **Objections and flow.** Skim headings alone. Place the strongest doubt and decisive price, responsibilities, fit, or timing before the step that depends on them. Treat inferred doubts as hypotheses. Cut repeated answers before cutting necessary qualifications.
6. **Action and completion.** Trace the first click, form or checkout, and confirmation in the available flow. Check what the visitor gives, gets, and does next. Use documented steps when the flow cannot be inspected and name any unresolved live check. Never submit a live form or reserve a time without explicit authorization.

Fix the earliest break in the buyer's expectation and rerun affected checks. Example: an 80% increase in enquiries fails as evidence for “fill your next batch 80% faster.” Keep the result within its measured scope and explain what the service actually does. A form before a scheduler needs copy explaining both steps.

For evaluation after publication, use the campaign outcome such as qualified bookings, paid enrollments, or purchases. Use clicks and submissions to diagnose drop-off. Compare traffic sources where possible.
