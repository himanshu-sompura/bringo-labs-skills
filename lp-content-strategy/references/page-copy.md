# Write the landing page

Use the discovery brief to choose one primary buyer, one promise, and one conversion action. Preserve any approved messaging and required section order.

## Choose the message

- Match the promise that brought visitors to the page. Use the ad, search intent, or outreach message to decide what belongs in the hero.
- Name the offer or audience and the outcome the buyer cares about. Explain what the offer actually does to help deliver that outcome.
- Give the buyer one concrete reason to choose this offer over what they do today. Use a relevant difference in the process, scope, terms, or results that the business can substantiate.
- Use customer language for the situation and stakes. Match each consequential claim to proof that supports that specific claim.

## Build the section order

Write an internal list of the questions a buyer must answer before acting. Give each section one question to resolve and place the strongest relevant proof near the claim or objection it addresses.

| Buyer question | What to write |
|---|---|
| Is this for me? What do I get? | A hero that names the audience or offer, states the outcome, and shows the primary action. |
| How would this work for me? | A concrete explanation of the process, deliverables, or first step. |
| Why should I believe it? | A relevant result, testimonial, demonstration, or example. Include context that affects how to interpret it. |
| Am I a fit? What will it cost or take? | The terms, requirements, or trade-offs that could change the buyer’s decision. |
| What happens if I act? | A specific CTA and a short explanation of what happens next. |

Treat the table as a menu. For high-intent search traffic, put the matching offer and action early. For cold outreach, explain the offer and establish credibility before asking for a higher-commitment action. Include an FAQ only for decision-making questions that still need an answer.

## Write paste-ready copy

Write in short, scannable blocks. Use headings that communicate the answer, bullets for distinct details, and concrete language the buyer recognizes. Let each heading pick up the next buyer question. Keep one primary action across the page and use button labels that accurately describe it.

**Example for outreach to cohort founders**

- **Headline** — See which Google Ads enquiries become paid seats
- **Supporting copy** — Get Search ads and a dedicated page for your next live cohort, with tracking from the first enquiry to paid enrollment.
- **Button** — Plan My Next Batch
- **Below the button** — Tell us about your next cohort, then pick a time to speak with Himanshu.

Deliver one final version in page order, including proof captions, form labels, and confirmation copy when they support the conversion path. Keep strategy notes and material assumptions outside the copy.
