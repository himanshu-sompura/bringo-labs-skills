# Natural transitions for landing pages

Use when two adjacent sections feel disconnected or a transition delays the next useful detail.

- **Read across the break.** Read the last line of one section and the next heading together. If the connection is clear, move straight to the heading. If it feels abrupt, rewrite the heading to pick up a specific detail from the previous section and move the decision forward.
- **Name the connection.** When the relationship matters, name it in the copy. Link a result to the claim it supports, a step to what it unlocks, or a requirement to what the buyer must do. Replace vague references like “this” or “that” when the heading needs to make sense to someone skimming.
- **Put the payoff in the heading.** If a line merely announces proof, pricing, or a process, put the actual result, price, or first step in the heading. Use a question only if the buyer would ask it and the next line answers it.

**Example**  
Section ends with “Each enquiry is tracked through to payment.”  
Next heading becomes “See which searches led to paid seats” in place of “The results.”
