# Landing page discovery

Read the brief, offer, entry message, and customer evidence first. Reuse confirmed answers. Ask up to three high-impact questions at a time, in plain language. Follow up only when the answer could change the audience, promise, section order, supporting proof, or CTA.

## Questions to resolve

| Find out | Ask when missing |
|---|---|
| Buyer and trigger | Who is the primary buyer for this page? What happened that made them look for help now, and what outcome do they want? |
| Traffic and intent | Where are they coming from? What did they search for, or what did the ad, referral, or outreach message lead them to expect? |
| Offer and alternatives | What do they actually get? What are the important terms or fit requirements? What would they do instead, and why would they choose this offer? |
| Objections and proof | What is the strongest reason they would hesitate? What verified evidence can we show that addresses it? |
| Action and expectations | What single action should they take? What happens immediately afterward, and what does the buyer need to know before committing? |

## Use customer evidence

Pull a few useful phrases from supplied sales calls, reviews, support threads, or messages. Look for how buyers describe the trigger, desired outcome, and hesitation. Keep customer statements, founder assumptions, and verified claims distinct. Preserve approved wording and any required section order.

## Decide when to write

Proceed when the primary buyer, entry context, offer, and action are clear enough to draft. Use the strongest supported promise. If proof is unavailable, narrow the claim or omit it. If a secondary detail is unknown, continue and flag it only if it materially affects the copy.

Carry forward one internal brief with the buyer and trigger, entry message, core promise, main objection, usable proof, and CTA with its next step. Use it to guide the page.
