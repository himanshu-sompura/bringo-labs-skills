# Landing page discovery

Use when a missing answer could change the audience, headline, section order, proof, or CTA. Read supplied materials first and ask up to three focused questions.

| Item | Ask when missing | Best available source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer and trigger | Who is it for? What happened? What did they try, and what do they need now? | Recent good-fit buyer calls or replies. Compare won and stalled decisions when possible. |
| Traffic | What did visitors see or search for before arriving? | Exact ad, search terms, referral, or outreach message. |
| Offer and fit | What do they get? How does it work, what does it cost, and who qualifies? What would they do instead? | Current approved terms, scope, and examples of delivery. |
| Doubt and proof | What might stop a qualified buyer? What evidence directly addresses it? | Lost or stalled enquiries, testimonials with permission, verified outcomes, or a real sample. |
| Action | What happens immediately? What must the buyer know before committing? | Live or documented form, booking, purchase, or follow-up path. |

Record **source and context → observed words or action → interpretation → copy decision**. Give independent buyers separate weight. Treat a search term as evidence of the query and competitor reviews as leads to investigate. Confirm permission before publishing identifiable quotes. If a trigger is inferred, use a verified offer-led opening and flag the assumption if material.

When sources conflict, check their date, segment, campaign and approval status. Use the current authoritative source where identifiable. Ask about a material conflict that would change the promise or commitment. If the fact can be omitted without misleading visitors or breaking the entry promise, draft around it and flag it when needed.

Stop once buyer, entry context, offer and next action are clear enough to write. When the user requests a draft with incomplete inputs, make supported progress and label unresolved essentials.
