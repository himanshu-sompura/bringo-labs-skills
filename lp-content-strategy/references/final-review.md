# Review the landing page copy

Review the draft against the discovery brief, the ad or outreach message, and the available proof. Fix each failed check before delivery. Preserve approved wording and section order. Flag material conflicts that need the user's decision.

## Run the buyer comprehension test

Read only the hero and primary CTA, as a first-time visitor would. Answer these questions using the visible copy.

1. Is this for someone like me?
2. What am I being offered, and what would I get from it?
3. What should I do next, and what happens when I do?

If any answer requires guessing, rewrite the headline, supporting copy, or CTA. Read the rest of the page and check whether it gives the buyer a credible reason to choose the offer.

## Fix what weakens the page

| Check | Fix when it fails |
|---|---|
| **Message match** | Carry the specific promise or intent from the ad, search, or outreach into the hero. |
| **Specific value** | Replace vague phrases such as “unlock growth” with an outcome the buyer cares about and a concrete detail about how the offer helps. |
| **Proof and accuracy** | Check numbers, terms, and claims against the source. Put the evidence near the claim. Narrow or remove unsupported claims. A rise in enquiries supports a claim about enquiries. |
| **Buyer objections** | Answer the strongest remaining doubt where it arises. Surface price, fit, effort, or timing when it materially affects the decision. |
| **Section flow** | Skim the headings alone. Give each section one clear job. Cut repeated benefits, throat-clearing, and paragraphs that merely link sections. |
| **CTA and follow-through** | Use one primary action throughout. Match the button label and nearby copy to the actual next step. Check that the form and confirmation carry through on that expectation. |

Return one paste-ready draft in page order. Place any material assumptions or missing facts in a brief note after the copy.
