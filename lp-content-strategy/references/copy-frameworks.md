# Landing page copy frameworks

Use after discovery, while drafting or fixing a weak section. Start with the entry message and the buyer’s biggest unresolved question. Apply only the patterns that help answer it. Keep framework names out of the page copy.

## 1. Choose the lead by what the buyer knows

| What the visitor arrives knowing                 | How to write the opening                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| They feel the problem                            | **PAS** — Name the trigger, show one credible consequence, then introduce how the offer helps. Keep the consequence proportional to the evidence.               |
| They know the outcome or are comparing solutions | **Outcome → mechanism** — Lead with the result or capability they want. Use the subhead to name the offer and explain the concrete step that makes it possible. |
| They came for a specific offer                   | **Offer → decision detail** — Name the offer first and surface the detail they need to act, such as price, eligibility, or start date.                          |

Carry the specific promise from the ad, search intent, or outreach into the first screen. If the headline needs context, let the subhead supply it.

## 2. Turn the difference into a benefit

**Buyer’s current alternative → distinct capability → practical payoff.** Name what they actually use or do today, including doing it manually. Identify a relevant difference in the offer, then ask, “What can the buyer now see, do, or decide?” Write that answer. If it still sounds generic, add the specific step or deliverable that enables it.

## 3. Match the evidence to the doubt

| Buyer’s doubt                                   | What to put beside the claim                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Will this work in my situation?                 | A relevant result with its metric, timeframe, and buyer context, when available.   |
| What will I actually get?                       | A demo, sample, or annotated screenshot. Label illustrative examples.              |
| What will it cost me in money, time, or effort? | The confirmed price or requirement, including who does the work when that matters. |

When evidence is limited, narrow the claim to what it supports. A sample report can show what gets tracked. A result claim needs measured outcomes.

## 4. Carry the action through

**Button → immediate next step → confirmation.** Label the real action. Add the one detail that resolves hesitation at the click, such as a form before booking or when a reply is due. After the action, confirm what happened and give the next step or verified timing.

## Example

Fictional invoice reporting tool for finance teams comparing software.

- **First draft** — Streamline accounts receivable with powerful reporting.
- **Headline** — See which invoices are overdue before your Monday review.
- **Subhead** — Connect your billing account to get a weekly report grouped by customer and days overdue.
- **CTA** — Preview the sample report
- **Proof caption** — Sample report with fictional invoice data. See how unpaid balances are grouped by customer and days overdue.
