# Mandatory discovery

Adapt these ten questions to the actual outcome and supplied context. Each must resolve a distinct decision; confirm known context rather than ignoring it. Ask at least ten, even for a small project, and add follow-ups until complete.

1. **Outcome and purpose:** What specific change should this project achieve, for whom, and why now?
2. **Success evidence:** What measurable result or observable test means success, what is the baseline, and where will evidence come from?
3. **Starting point:** What is already done/available, what has been tried, and what failed or remains unfinished?
4. **Scope:** Which deliverables are essential, and what is explicitly excluded?
5. **Time:** Is there a fixed deadline, preferred window, external date, or waiting period? "No fixed deadline" is valid.
6. **Capacity and ownership:** Who will do the work, how much focused time is available, what skills exist, and who decides or approves?
7. **Resources:** What budget, tools, access, assets, or outside support are available or restricted? Confirm if no budget is needed.
8. **Requirements and quality:** Which audience needs, functional requirements, examples, standards, and non-negotiables must be satisfied?
9. **Risks and constraints:** What could block success, which external people/systems matter, and which premises need validation?
10. **Execution and delivery:** Is the output a reviewable plan or authorized ClickUp creation, what destination/names should be used if publishing, and what task sizes/tracking needs suit the executor?

Invite supporting documents, links, current work, examples, past results, and existing ClickUp structure. Do not require documents that do not exist or read unrelated private sources. Read current source contents rather than trusting remembered versions.

Ask additional questions about any applicable unknowns: audience, metric definition, access, cost allocation, reviewers, destination, conflicting dates, strategy choices, or tool constraints.

## Handle incomplete answers

Maintain a compact requirement ledger when useful: requirement, actual answer/source, and Answered / Incomplete / Unanswered. This is a discovery record, not a project plan.

- Retain answered items; ask only focused follow-ups.
- For "get more clients," clarify target, baseline, timeframe, offer, channels/constraints, capacity, and resources as applicable.
- For "make it better," identify the failure and observable improvement.
- For "I don't know," offer concrete choices and help decide, then wait.
- For conflicting constraints, explain the conflict and ask which may change.
- Treat explicit exclusion or "not applicable" as a completed decision; do not keep asking.
- Do not demand certainty about future market behavior. Clarify the target, test, measurement, limits, and decision rules instead.
- Do not ask ceremonial questions just to reach ten; make each question useful for this project.

## Gate

Proceed only after at least ten substantive discovery questions have been asked and answered, all applicable areas are complete, supporting context is considered, contradictions are resolved, and no requirement is incomplete. Do not substitute assumptions, placeholders, or generic discovery tasks.

A benchmark example helps tailor this skill but is not mandatory for creating the skill itself. The intake governs planning an actual project, not editing these instructions.
