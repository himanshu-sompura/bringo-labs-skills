# Landing page wireframe decisions

Use this reference when a placement choice, proof limitation, disclosure, or action path needs more than the core workflow. Treat the expected effects as hypotheses to check against the actual copy and audience.

## Place information where it resolves uncertainty

| Visitor signal | Layout response | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The visitor arrived with a specific need from an ad, referral, or message. | Put the matching audience, use case, and offer in the opening text. Make the promised next step visible or easy to find. | The visitor can connect the page to what earned their click. |
| The offer sounds desirable but abstract. | Put one mechanism, sample deliverable, or annotated walkthrough near the promise. Label invented data as illustrative. | The visitor can explain how the service works using its actual steps. |
| The visitor understands the offer and doubts the result. | Bring relevant, authorized proof forward. Place the measured outcome, comparison period, source, and material limits together. | The visitor describes the result at the same level of certainty and scope as the evidence. |
| The decision depends on fit, cost, or workload. | Use visible eligibility, cost, and ownership groups before the form or checkout. Align comparable responsibilities under explicit owner labels. Keep labels with their entries on mobile. | The visitor can find their total commitment and explain who does what. |
| One component competes with the headline or action. | Reduce its weight, move it beside the claim it explains, or remove it if it adds no information. Use scale, contrast, alignment and spacing deliberately. | The first, second and third things a visitor notices match what they need to decide. |

When a first-screen choice is uncertain, sketch two options with real wording at the same mobile width. For example, a service page can lead with a process sample for visitors who cannot picture the service or a directly relevant, permitted outcome for visitors who know the service and doubt its efficacy. Hold the offer and action steady. Pick one based on the best available evidence or use the trade-off to define the next usability task.

Use proximity and consistent alignment to show which things belong together. Make sequence, comparison and priority explicit in the layout. Check them after stacking. A diagram should answer a specific question, such as who updates a record at each step. Use an annotated sample only when visitors can distinguish it from a live customer result.

## Make persuasion inspectable

- **Social proof.** Choose the most relevant customer, use case or measured outcome with verified publication rights. Keep the label, context and qualification at skimming distance from the number. Keep unauthorized names, logos, and metrics whose meaning cannot be established out of the visitor frame. Record the gap in the handoff and use an independently supported result when available. Label an empty proof slot only when its placement needs review. A lead-volume result cannot support an enrolment claim. Hold any necessary changes to approved copy for review.
- **Risk and commitment.** Make next-step cost, effort, ongoing terms, and responsibilities easy to find. Explain the actual reversibility, trial or cancellation terms if provided. Put purchase-critical terms in the ordinary reading path.
- **Urgency and comparisons.** Use a real deadline, capacity constraint, or comparable baseline when the offer supplies one. State the basis in place. Exclude invented scarcity, inflated comparison prices, fake social signals, and controls that obscure an alternative or an exit.
- **Choice and disclosure.** Give the intended action a clear hierarchy. Retain secondary paths that answer a visitor need or are required by the brief. Use disclosure for technical depth or secondary questions. Keep essential facts and limitations in the main reading flow.
- **Familiar controls.** Give links, buttons and fields recognizable affordances. Repeat the primary action at points where a visitor might be ready. Add a sticky action only if repeated inline controls leave a demonstrated access problem and the sticky element can coexist with narrow screens, zoom, focus, and the on-screen keyboard.

## Connect the CTA to reality

Map the applicable states and label them for the reviewer. State and destination are separate decisions. A booking link can be available on success, sent later, or absent. Only show an instant calendar if that is the specified path.

| State | What to show |
| --- | --- |
| Before the click | Action label, nearby commitment, and the next step when it is otherwise ambiguous. |
| Input and review | Visible labels, needed help and optionality. Record each field's use in the handoff. Keep the submitted terms or selection available for review where they affect commitment. |
| Pending | Confirm work is in progress. Identify the result that would end waiting. Prevent duplicate submission in interactive prototypes where the control simulates a pending request. |
| Correctable validation error | Explain the fix beside the field, link a summary to affected inputs when several errors can occur, give the state a useful focus destination, and preserve safe entries. |
| Service failure or unavailable path | Explain the state and offer the available retry or alternative. Preserve appropriate entries where supported. Distinguish unavailability and eligibility decisions from bad input. |
| Success and follow-on | Name the event that actually occurred, what happens next, and any verified timing. If a subsequent action is required, show its route and separate success state. |

## Check decisions without leading the visitor

| Task | Observable pass | Repair if it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start at the top. Explain what is offered and who it could help. | The visitor names the offer and intended audience in their own words. | Revisit entry-message match, first-screen text priority and mobile crop. |
| Decide whether this would work for your situation. Find the evidence or terms you need. | The visitor finds the relevant fit condition, commitment or evidence and interprets it accurately. | Move the missing detail toward the decision, or restore its visual association with the claim. |
| Take the next step when you feel ready. Say what you expect before clicking. | The visitor finds the intended control, anticipates the actual next state, and can recover from an applicable error. | Change action placement, next-step cue, state labels or recovery. |

Use representative participants for observed usability. An internal walkthrough checks consistency and obvious friction. Live performance needs its own measurement of the relevant outcome, such as qualified enquiries, bookings, or paid conversions.
